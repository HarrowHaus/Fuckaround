# SIX FEET IS NOT ENOUGH — Vocalist's Map

**The concept:** one long breakdown. Seven movements, no verses, no choruses — your
voice is the lead instrument and the recurring callout is the chorus substitute.
**Key:** G# minor, staircasing DOWN: G#1 (mvts I–II) → F#1 (III–V) → **F1 (final form)** — one-way ratchet
**Tempo staircase:** 84 → 72 → rit 68/64 → **62 quarter-time** → 56 outro
**Runtime:** ~4:20
**Track against:** `mix/six_feet_is_not_enough_vocal_ready.wav` (−6 dBFS, no limiter)

The whole song obeys the anchor law: beat 1 and the backbeat are sacred, all violence
happens between them. The gaps are YOUR turns — negative space here is literally
engineered as the other instrument's slot, and that instrument is you.

## Movement map

| Time | Movement | Feel | What's happening | Vocal role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Intro | free | Feedback + three lone unaccompanied chugs (the bookend motif), pick-scrape riser | One dry spoken/growled line between the chugs — you open the song, not the band |
| 0:11 | I — BOUNCE | 84 half-time, swung | 3+3+2 grids, china quarters, snare 3; mutations every 4 bars; 32nd stutter bars at 9 and 11 | Mid gutturals riding the bounce. Land syllables ON the anchors, leave the gaps empty. Bar 12's quarter-stop = one bark into the hole |
| 0:46 | II — LURCH | 84, snare on 4 | Unequal-gap grids that stumble; tom answers in the gaps; b9 cluster ring; **CALLOUT at 1:14** — drums drop out, one dry chug per beat | The stumble wants long held lows over bars 1–2, short jabs bars 3–4. The callout is THE hook line — shout it a cappella over the lone chugs, twice |
| 1:20 | III — SLUDGE | 72 (drop #1), pitch → F# | Ring-out–anchored sludge-bounce, choir enters underneath, 32nd stutters creep in bar 9+ | Slower, lower. Let rings breathe; your lowest sustained gutturals live here |
| 2:00 | IV — SLAM | 72, ride-bell, swung | The swagger detour: slam groove, dead-note scrapes, audible bass; ends on a **full-bar choke-stop, only the 808 tail survives** | Slam-style rhythmic gutturals ("bree" territory legal). At the stop: ONE word, then let the sub shake the room |
| 2:20 | V — THE VALLEY | 72, no cymbals | Everything strips: 2 bars drums-only → bass drone + lone chugs in oceans of space, floor toms, distant feedback | Whisper. Spoken word. Breathing. This is the horror-movie pocket — the quieter you go, the harder 3:01 hits |
| 2:47 | FALSE ENDING | rit 68→64 | Ring-out + fall + feedback, then near-silence with a single 808 tail and a riser | Nothing. Or one gasp. Make them think it's over |
| 3:01 | **FINAL FORM** | **62 quarter-time, root drops to F1** | Impact + double 808s, sparsest/biggest grid, orchestral stabs on anchors only, china locked to the hits, everything played late (behind the kick); 1 bar of imported blast at 3:32; **full-band stop at 3:36 — a-cappella hole**; rebuild nastier; b9 ring + squeal + two-octave dive at 3:56 | The peak of the vocal. Quarter-time = each hit ~2s apart: one syllable per impact, inhuman noises in the gaps. The 3:36 hole is your a-cappella moment — the single most important line of the song. Freak-out over the dive |
| 4:03 | Outro | 56 | Density halves each bar → one final lone chug + the intro's feedback pitch (bookend) | Last whispered line as the feedback dies. You close the song, same as you opened it |

## Writing notes
- **The callout line recurs**: 1:14 (over dry chugs), 3:36 (fully a cappella), optionally
  whispered in the valley. Three appearances, three intensities — that's the song's hook.
- IOI design: final form impacts land ~1.9s apart — crowd-stomp tempo. Phrase AT the
  impacts, not between them; the emptiness is doing the heavy lifting.
- Title/lyric lane: burial that doesn't hold, weight that keeps coming — the pitch and
  tempo staircases literally dig downward; write the lyric to descend with them.
